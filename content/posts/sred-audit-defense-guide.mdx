## What Triggers an SR&ED Audit?

CRA selects claims for review through a combination of risk-based scoring and random selection. Claims that are larger than average for the company's sector, claims with high contractor costs relative to employee costs, first-time claimants, and companies in industries CRA has identified as high-risk are all more likely to face scrutiny. The audit rate has increased meaningfully over the past three years as CRA tightens administration of the program.

## What Actually Happens During a Review

An SR&ED review (the formal term for what most people call an audit) is initiated when CRA assigns a Financial Review Officer (FRO) and a Research and Technology Advisor (RTA) to your file. The FRO examines the financial side - payroll records, contractor agreements, expense documentation. The RTA examines the technical side - your project descriptions, hypotheses, test records, and technical narratives.

The review can be conducted by correspondence (you submit documents), by phone interview, or in person at your office. The RTA may want to speak directly with members of your technical team. This is one reason we always recommend coaching your engineers before any CRA interaction - not to obscure anything, but to ensure they communicate the technical work in the language and framing CRA expects.

## The Most Common Audit Failure Points

In our experience, SR&ED audits most commonly fail on three fronts:

**1. Inadequate contemporaneous documentation.** CRA expects records created at the time of the work - not reconstructed after the fact. If your engineers cannot produce time logs, test results, or project notes from the period in question, the RTA will reduce or deny the claim.

**2. Weak technical narrative.** A technical narrative that describes what was built rather than why it was uncertain, what was hypothesized, and what was learned will not survive technical scrutiny. The narrative must document the technological uncertainty, the systematic investigation, and the advancement - in those terms.

**3. Overclaiming on contractor costs.** CRA applies a 65% inclusion rate to arm's-length contractor costs and requires that contractors have been doing SR&ED work in Canada. Companies that claim 100% of contractor invoices without proper analysis are a common audit target.

The best audit defense happens before you submit the claim.
